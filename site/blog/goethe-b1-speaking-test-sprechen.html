The Goethe B1 speaking test is short, but many candidates dread it: you plan with a partner you may not know, present alone, then answer questions. This guide covers how it works, how it is scored and how to prepare, with German phrases you can use. It builds on the speaking approach we use at Fluency AI.

Key takeaways

  • It has three parts: plan something together, present a topic, then react and answer questions.[1][3]

What is the Goethe B1 speaking test?

The Goethe-Zertifikat B1 speaking test (Sprechen) is the oral module of the B1 exam. Usually two candidates take it together for about 15 minutes, after 15 minutes of preparation. You plan something together, give a short presentation and answer questions. It is scored out of 100, and 60 points passes the module.[1]

Many people take it because B1 is the level German naturalisation asks for. City authorities refer to the “prescribed B1 level” and look for a B1 certificate.[4][5] Since 1 November 2026 the DTZ also needs at least A2 in every part, with B1 in speaking, but Goethe B1 follows its own rules.[6][7]

How the test works, step by step

  1. Preparation (15 minutes). You receive the task sheets and stamped rough paper and prepare your notes.[1]
  2. Introduction (about 1 minute). Two examiners greet you, and one chats briefly with you. This is not graded.[1]
  3. Part 1, Gemeinsam etwas planen (2 to 3 minutes). You and your partner plan something together.[1]
  4. Part 2, Ein Thema präsentieren (3 to 4 minutes each). You take turns presenting a topic.[1]
  5. Part 3, Über ein Thema sprechen (1 to 2 minutes each). Your partner and the second examiner react to your presentation and ask questions, and you answer.[1]

What you do in each part

The three parts of the Goethe B1 speaking test
PartTimeWhat the task asks
1. Plan together2 to 3 minutesYou get four bullet points about a situation, such as planning a visit to someone in hospital. You discuss the points, make suggestions, react to your partner’s ideas and decide together.[2][3]
2. Present a topic3 to 4 minutes eachFive slides guide you through your talk: introduce the topic, explain its structure, report on your own situation or an experience, give an opinion with examples, and finish by thanking your listeners.[2][3]
3. React and answer1 to 2 minutes eachYou comment on your partner’s presentation and ask a question, then answer questions about your own.[1][3]

How it is scored and what you need to pass

Two examiners grade you separately against fixed criteria, out of 100. The scores are averaged and rounded, and the introduction is not graded.[1] The criteria sheets are in the examiner pages of Goethe’s free practice test, so read them before you practise.[1][2]

You pass the module with at least 60 points (60 percent).[1] Each module is scored on its own, and the speaking examiners are not told your written results.[1]

How to prepare: a four-week plan

This plan is our suggestion, not an official programme. Use Goethe’s free practice test for real task sheets.[2]

  1. Week 1: Part 1. Plan an everyday event with a partner, such as a trip or a gift: suggest, react, then decide, out loud.
  2. Week 2: Part 2. Build a five-slide template, then give three timed presentations of 3 to 4 minutes from keywords only.[1]
  3. Week 3: Part 3. Listen to a partner’s talk and ask one real question, then practise answering questions about your own topic.
  4. Week 4: full mock test. Take 15 minutes to prepare, then run all three parts in about 15 minutes. Record it, listen once and fix one thing.[1]

Three mistakes to avoid

  • Treating Part 1 as a monologue. You must react to your partner’s suggestions and decide together.[3]
  • Writing full sentences while preparing. Only brief key-point notes are allowed, and 15 minutes is short.[1]
  • Ignoring Part 3. You must also comment on your partner’s talk and ask a question.[1][3]

Questions people ask

How long is the Goethe B1 speaking test?

About 15 minutes for two candidates taking it together, or about 10 minutes as an individual exam. You also get 15 minutes to prepare before either format.[1]

How many points do I need to pass the Goethe B1 speaking test?

You need at least 60 out of 100 points (60 percent) to pass the speaking module.[1]

Can I use notes in the speaking test?

Yes, but only the brief key-point notes you made during the 15-minute preparation.[1] Write keywords, not full sentences.

Can I retake only the speaking module?

Yes. The four modules can be taken individually and retaken as often as the exam centre’s capacity allows.[1]

Does the new DTZ rule apply to Goethe B1?

No. The change that took effect on 1 November 2026 concerns the DTZ and the “Leben in Deutschland” test, while Goethe, telc and ÖSD B1 exams follow their own rules.[7]
